**The Paradox of the Scoreboard**

Now the detail that makes this round genuinely strange.

The damage was level — 0.82% apart. The points were not. The round finished **303 to 149**, better than two to one for the attackers. Damage counts what you did; points count *when* you did it, tick after tick, and on that ledger Lithuania was ahead at practically every reading. The tracker's own verdict, minutes before the close, was blunt: **"Lithuania extending the lead."**

The hit counts show how two sides reached the same total by opposite roads. At the last reading before the round shut, the attackers had landed **180,729** hits against the defenders' **164,488** — sixteen thousand fewer. But every defensive hit landed harder: **3,468** damage per hit against **3,181**, a **9%** edge per blow.

The defence has the sharper sword. The offensive has the faster arm — and the faster arm takes the ticks.

The human scale behind it is worth stating plainly: **2,757 individual fighters** swung for the attack, **2,598** for the defence. **5,355 people**, in one round, over one region.

**The Bill: 172,000 and Two Empty Treasuries**

Damage is the headline. Money is the part nobody publishes, and it is the part that decides how long this can last.

Bounty is the first tap. Lithuania paid **0.1** per thousand damage, Ukraine **0.086** — and between them they had already distributed **85,404** to the people doing the hitting: **41,484** out of the attacking pool, **43,920** out of the defending one.

Mercenary contracts are the second tap, and here Ukraine is spending like a nation that cannot afford to lose. **313 contracts** were awarded in this battle. Lithuania took **116** of them, for **40,193**. Ukraine bought **197**, for **47,016** — more contracts and more money than the side that started this war.

Add the two taps and round one alone burned **172,613** in war spending.

Now look at what is left in the vaults. Lithuania's treasury: **237.80**. Ukraine's treasury: **34.94**. Two nations that have just spent the better part of two hundred thousand between them are, at this moment, functionally broke. Round two has to be paid for out of income, not savings — and that, far more than the damage graph, is what will decide this battle.

**What Happens Next**

Muntenia broke a billion and held. Central Ukraine broke **1.161 billion** and fell — by 0.82%.

Lithuania leads **1–0**. It needs one more round. Ukraine needs two, out of a treasury with **34.94** in it, against a coalition that just proved it can field a hundred and ten nations and still not be the side with the harder punch.

Round two is live. If it burns like the first one, this single region will cost the world more than two billion damage and a third of a million in bounty and contracts — for one tile of plains on a map that has seven hundred and twenty-six of them.
