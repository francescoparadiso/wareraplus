Votes and Agreements, explained straight: what they do, who can use them, and why every foreign minister should be writing pacts there.

WarEra+ has two tools for the political side of the game: Votes and Agreements. People have started using them, so here is how they really work, no fluff. Numbers read on 11 November 2026. By , who builds the tool. Open WarEra+, it's free.

Votes: decide things outside the game, in secret if you want

The game has laws, and congress votes them in game. What it doesn't give you is a way to vote on anything outside the game: a treaty, a stance, a motion. And a Discord poll doesn't work, because everyone sees who voted what. Votes (a button inside Political) fills that hole. It lives on WarEra+, not in the game, so it binds only whoever agrees to respect it. Role play, but with proper rules.

The basics

  • Who opens a vote: the government or the congress of a nation. You sign in with Discord and link your character (a vote counts per character).
  • Who votes: you pick one: congress, congress + government, or a referendum for every citizen. For congress the voters are a snapshot taken when the vote opens, so an election halfway through can't change them. In a referendum you must be a citizen now, level 10+, with a character created before the vote opened.
  • Your ballot: in favour, against, abstain, or spoiled (a spoiled ballot needs a short note, read out during the count). It's final. The booth tells you which character you're voting as.
  • When it passes: simple majority, absolute majority (more than half of everyone entitled) or two thirds, plus an optional quorum from 1% to 100%. Abstentions and spoiled ballots count for the quorum, not for the majority. A vote lasts from 1 hour to 7 days, and can be scheduled up to 30 days ahead.
  • Who sees it: the text and result can be public or only for government and congress. That a vote exists, its title, dates and turnout are always public.

The count

While a vote is open you only see the turnout: partial results would sway whoever hasn't voted yet. When it closes, the count starts one minute later, at the same second for everyone. With an open ballot a hemicycle lights up seat by seat. With a secret ballot a ballot box opens and one ballot comes out every 3 seconds. If everyone entitled has voted, whoever opened the vote can start the count right away (10 seconds). The government can also set a Discord channel for notices: opening, count start, result.

The first real vote on the tool: Italy's congress, secret ballot, 11 of 17 members voted. Text and result are reserved, so this is all that's public. See it in WarEra+.

How a secret ballot stays secret

The server has to know who voted, to stop double votes, but not what. So the two things sit in two tables with no link between them, and neither has a timestamp. When the vote is sealed the ballots are shuffled and the rows are deleted. A secret vote never goes in the audit log either, because the log has the time. It's secret even from WarEra+ administrators.

Honest limit. It doesn't protect against someone with direct access to the server while the vote is open. And in a referendum the number of people entitled is an estimate, so there's no absolute majority there.

Agreements: one place for every pact

Until October the NAPs on the map came from a spreadsheet I updated by hand. Since 23 October the spreadsheet is gone: Agreements are the only source of NAPs on the map. A pact in force shows up by itself and disappears when it expires (the map rereads them every 10 minutes).

  • A pact has two sides, each a nation or an alliance. For an alliance, whoever joins is in and whoever leaves is out. You can add extra nations to a side (proxies, allies outside the alliance).
  • What you write: title, full text, start and end (empty end = no expiry), units left out of the pact, and an optional damage cap per battle for one side or both.
  • Everyone can read, no login. Writing is for the government (president, vice, ministers) of one of the nations involved, or of a member of an involved alliance. No alliance filter: a pact has two sides and the other one has to be able to write it too.

The check

Press Check on a pact and the tool rereads the battles of its period and flags two things: a direct attack (a nation of one side opened a battle against the other) and a cap exceeded (damage by one side's citizens against the other, in one battle, above the limit). Units left out are subtracted player by player.

The check on the Italy–Austria pact. Try it on any pact.

Honest limit: it's an alarm, not a judge. The check is an estimate, because the game doesn't tell the tool everything:

  • Units and nationalities are the ones of today, not of the day of the battle. If a player changed unit or nation since, the tool can't know.
  • In huge battles only the top 100 players are read, so the real damage can be higher than shown (the line says so when it happens).
  • A battle counts on the day it ended: one that started before the pact and finished after it still shows up.
  • A pact can cover core regions only (the original regions of the nations in it) or all regions, occupied ones included. With core only, battles in occupied regions are left out, and the check tells you how many.
  • Each check reads the last 90 days, 120 battles at most.

So a red line means "go read that battle and talk to the other government", not "caught".

A call to foreign ministers and congress members

This part is for you, ministers of foreign affairs and members of congress of every nation. The world has 181 nations. The archive has three pacts. Let's fix that.

Today, to know if two nations have a NAP, you ask on Discord or trust someone's memory. If every government wrote its pacts in one public place, anyone, even from a third nation, could open one page and see who agreed what, until when, with which cap. The map would draw them without a spreadsheet, expired pacts would stay as history, and "you broke the pact" would have a page both sides can read.

  • Foreign ministers: write your nation's pacts there, the old ones too. A few minutes: Discord, link your character, New agreement. Don't wait for the other side, either one can write it. When a pact is renewed or ends early, change the date.
  • Congress members: a treaty is a decision, so vote it with Votes before the government signs (open for a public record, secret to let members disagree freely). Then the minister registers it in Agreements. The two don't talk to each other automatically, but together they give you a vote and a published text.

WarEra+ enforces nothing: it records pacts and measures battles. What happens when a pact is broken is still between the two governments. It just makes sure they're reading the same page. If a pact type or a field you need is missing (for now only non-aggression pacts), tell me.
