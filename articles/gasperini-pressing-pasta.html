With great ideas comes great responsibility. And so it does with great systems of play.

As many of you know (as if my De Rossi profile picture weren't obvious enough), I'm a die-hard Roma fan, and today we're going to talk about a tactical subject: Gasperini's pressing.

The Man-Marker

Our very own Gian Piero does something that is, in truth, extremely simple. He marks. He marks suffocatingly, man to man: he comes to get you the moment you win the ball, without letting you turn, aggressively. He plays on the front foot, trying to destabilise the opponent's plans.

And that is exactly what did on Monday, with the lightning declaration of war to grab the priority over and the attack on Sfax.

Emptying the Midfield

Gasperini's game does one more extremely interesting thing: it empties the midfield. It leaves the centre empty, free, pushing men out to the flanks and dropping a midfielder onto the defensive line to play the ball, so that the opponent has to follow him, which frees the space behind them for the runs of Roma's wing-backs.

Which, if you like, is a bit like what we do when we wait for T3 to unleash our firepower, flipping fronts and striking fast. With 20M bursts in a net 47 seconds.

20M in less than a minute

The Limits of Gasperinism

But Gasperinism has its limits too, and they are just as simple. It requires that EVERYONE does their part. That the 1-on-1 man-marking doesn't break. It takes effort and concentration.

And in WarEra's diplomacy and geopolitics, the exact same thing is needed. An alliance has to mark another one, player on player, going to get it high up the pitch when needed, fighting 1-on-1, so that the clash is an even one that depends only on your own strength, and above all avoiding a double-team from the opponent. And that is vital for everyone.

Yesterday: Tripoli

Yesterday, exactly this happened. In , FORGE was left free to play, with no other demanding fronts, and whoever was supposed to go and get them simply didn't, leaving us to play outnumbered, alone against the world.

The result was anything but a foregone conclusion, and Italy and P.A.S.T.A. fought like lions, setting the record for damage in a single battle in our history.

But, as in real-life stories, effort and valour don't always translate into results on the battlefield, or on the pitch.

Today Us, Tomorrow You

Yesterday it was our turn. Yesterday we found ourselves succumbing to an overwhelming force, because the markings and the schemes broke down.

Because someone didn't do their duty. And that's fine: we take note and draw the due conclusions.

But tomorrow? Tomorrow, when it's P.A.S.T.A. that loses its marker, what will happen?

Today us, tomorrow you.
Either victory, together, or defeat, gentlemen. There is no other way.
