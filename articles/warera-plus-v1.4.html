WarEra+ v1.4 — from plan to war, from vote to tournament

It's frappa10, and this post is long for a simple reason: it's been a while since v1.1, and in the meantime the game changed its skin. The v0.27 update removed the labour market, brought employees and government types, and moved half the tool from one place to another. So this round has two halves: what I had to put back on its feet, and what I got to build from scratch in the meantime.

If I had to pick one thread for this version: WarEra+ no longer only lets you watch. Until now it was a window onto the game — the map, the numbers, the history. Now you can also organise things: plan an operation, sign a pact, vote, follow a tournament. All of it stays outside the real game (it touches nothing of yours), but it's the first time the tool helps you decide, not just understand.

First of all: v0.27, put back in order

The game changed its rules halfway through, and plenty of things the tool showed stopped making sense. I rewrote them instead of letting them limp along:

  • Production bonus became Daily income. The map view that coloured the world by bonus now colours it by what a region actually earns in a day, with the nations ranking and the most profitable regions. In each nation's panel, in place of bonus and ethics, you'll find income and government type.
  • The Industrial Optimizer was rebuilt. With no labour market, the old tabs (Position, Workers, Hiring) had nothing left to say. There are now two, written on the numbers the game measures day by day: Companies — what a point is worth and costs, the margin over the last seven days, and signals for those losing money or standing idle — and Skills, to spread your points across Companies, Energy, Production and Management.
  • Government battle bonuses are in the tool: the patriotic bonus of the Nationalist State, the Federation and the Plutocracy, the Patriotic Front's bunker bonus, the Mercenary State's double orders. You'll find them where the ethics used to be.
  • Names on the map use the nations' colours as in the game: the game stopped handing them to us ready-made, so I derive them from the nation's colour, lightened to stay readable.
  • Production yields, Nation stats, Political, play style, borders and My nation were adapted to the new way of calculating. The Guide explains all of this in plain words.

Operations: the war-plan whiteboard

This is the biggest addition, and it comes from something anyone who has organised a war knows: the plan lives in a chat, in a screenshot and in the heads of three people.

The private area now has an Operations section. It's a whiteboard: each operation is a sequence of steps with a time, joined by arrows, and with the "See on map" button the plan opens on the real map, region by region. The government writes the plans; anyone with access to the nation can move a step's status with a click — done, in progress, skipped.

What I like most is that it knows the game. You can attach already-open battles to the plan (even other nations' battles), and the board shows when they'll end, with an estimate calibrated on past battles for the uncertain cases. Pills are drawn as the chart background — the 8 hours of effect and the 15 and a half of malus — so you see right away when an attack window closes. There's the priority timer, with the rule that you can't have more than one battle at a time against the same state, and the extra hour after a conquest. Battles are coloured by who fights them: blue ours, green the alliance's, red the enemies'.

To avoid the blank page there's an auto-builder that proposes a draft, and you work directly on the chart: quick add, drag, arrows. Everything saves by itself, and battles and the plan refresh every two minutes, so if a teammate changes something you see it without reloading.

One thing to say clearly: Operations is part of My nation, so only nations with access to the private area can see it, not everybody.

Agreements: non-aggression pacts finally have a home

Until now the NAPs on the map came from a sheet someone updated by hand. It was the most fragile part of the whole tool, and I knew it.

The new Agreements section is the archive of pacts: nation with nation, nation with alliance, alliance with alliance, each with its text, start and end dates, the units left out and, if there is one, a damage cap per battle. Each side can have extra nations added (useful for proxies and for allies outside the alliance). Pacts in force become NAPs on the map by themselves, and disappear by themselves when they expire.

And there's a check: the section flags direct attacks between the two sides and exceeded caps, summing damage by nationality on the opposite side. It's an estimate, and says so, but it's the kind of thing that used to be discovered after the argument.

Reading them is public; writing them can be done by the government of one of the nations involved — because a pact has two sides, and the counterpart has to be able to write it too. At the top of the list there are the numbers: pacts in the archive, in force, nations involved.

Votes: a real ballot box inside Political

Political has a new button, Votes. The government (or congress) can open a vote, and only congress, congress plus government or all citizens in a referendum can cast it. Your choice of open or secret ballot.

The count isn't a table that just appears. In an open vote a hemicycle lights up live, with votes going to their seat one at a time; in a secret vote there's a ballot box from which one ballot at a time is drawn, flips over and flies to its pile. It's theatre, I know, but a vote you watch being counted is a vote you trust more. When everyone has voted, whoever opened it can show the count right away.

The government can set a Discord channel for notifications: opening, count start, result. Voting needs the Discord login with your linked character. And on the secret ballot, a promise worth making: the system doesn't know who voted what, by the way it's built, not because it says so.

Tournaments: who reaches the final, before they do

A new Insights entry, Tournaments, with two tabs: Military units and Nations, each on the most recent tournament of its kind. The bracket — the units one has nine rounds and 457 teams — is split into sectors, and you can pick a team (also with "My unit") to see its path round by round and, for every future round, who it could face, strongest first, with a difficulty band.

And then the probabilities: for each match, the chance of winning; and along the bracket, how likely it is to reach each round and win the tournament. A team's strength is the last seven days' damage of the tournament's entrants only, and also takes into account how it played in the matches already done. There's also a "What if…?": pick the winners yourself and the downstream probabilities recompute.

I want to be honest about the predictions: the tool writes them as soon as a match has both teams and never touches them again, and in the ranking you see how many it got right. In the October MU tournament the favourite won 250 matches out of 329. It's not magic, it's a model that tells you every day how wrong it was.

Battles: from watching to reliving

  • The battle replay now has the wall going back and forth as in the game's own view, with territories and units, and an animation at the end of the round. You can also replay from the archive, not just from battles in progress.
  • The trend is a chart you can really explore: zoom, pan, box, expand. Battle timeline and bounty timeline are two separate tabs, with nation names.
  • Money spent has its own section, per side, with toggles to switch items on and off. Tables can be sorted.
  • Round and battle end are written in the list and above the map markers, so you see at a glance which battles are about to close.

In the same round: charts are explorable everywhere. Hover or tap and the value appears — in parliament, war expenses, the donuts, player wealth. It's one of those things that look small and change how you use every view. Damage over time now has an average and a long history, and political history shows the members of all parties.

The map has ships

Sea routes now really cross the sea, not the land, and ships move smoothly on a separate layer, at a speed I tweaked several times until it looked right at world view. It's the least useful thing on this whole list and one of my favourites. The particle background, meanwhile, adapts its number of nodes and lines to your screen, so it doesn't make your phone stutter.

Units and nations: better comparisons

  • The 1 vs 1 comparison is now one thing for everything — units, nations, alliances — with the same way of reading numbers, and for units there's damage over time: how they did week after week, not just today.
  • Per member and per fighter: in the units list a column with weekly damage divided by members, sortable, and in the unit page the average over only those who actually hit. A 50-person unit and a 500-person one can finally be compared.
  • Damage and devcore in nation and alliance stats, and in the alliance popup a Dmg/Core column per nation. Long tables scroll in a box with an always visible bar.
  • In the nation panel on laptops there's less to click through (the tooltip no longer pins, it duplicated the panel) and the third button is now called Statistics and opens the right tab.

Other tools: the community's work

One thing I didn't write myself, and I'm glad to put here. The WarEra community has made wonderful things, and there was no place to find them. Now there is: a new Insights entry, Other tools, with one card per site — EMPRESS, SPYWarera, Unikhorne WarEra Suite, Arcana Era — with a line on what it does and the link, which opens in a new tab. They're third-party tools, not affiliated with WarEra+, and the page says so at the top. If you've made one too, write to me.

And one thing that was broken

As always, honesty: borders watch the bases and bunkers of regions and warn when something is turned on or built. There was a case where, if none of the checked buildings existed, the whole group was discarded as an error, and those regions were never read: a base built there later would have raised no alert. It was a real hole. It's closed.

Thanks, now go try it

wareraplus.vercel.app — free, works from your phone, and the information-only parts (map, stats, battles, tournaments, agreements) don't even ask for a login. If this is your first time hearing about WarEra+, start from Part 1: the map is the front door.

A practical note: some of the new things (Agreements, Votes, Operations, Tournaments) fill up over time, because the game doesn't keep history and the tool collects it day by day. If a section looks empty on day one, that's normal, and it usually says so itself at the top.
